## The challenge

The **Sunstone Economic Development Challenge @ USC Price** pairs graduate teams with a real public client.
Our client is the **City of Ontario, California** (Economic Development Agency). The deliverable is a
proposal of ranges, funding sources and explicit assumptions rather than false precision. Under the
challenge template it must state a budget, name who pays, and say how success will be measured.

Our proposal, *Connecting Ontario: Building the Inland Empire's Next Generation Economy*, comes from
team members Danny Valencia, Haley Chavez, Emily Schoof and Nathaniel Gallardo (USC Price MPA/MPP).

## The problem

Ontario does not lack economic activity. It has:

| Asset | Scale (as cited in the proposal) |
|---|---|
| Ontario International Airport (ONT) | 7M+ passengers; ~$4.8B regional output and ~24,300 jobs (2024) |
| Ontario Mills | ~12M visits a year |
| Toyota Arena / Capital City | ~1.2M guests a year |
| Ontario Sports Empire | ~1.2M projected visitors a year (City projection) |
| Logistics / industrial base | One of Southern California's largest; FTZ 50; HUB@ONT tenants |
| Housing growth | Ontario Ranch |
| Transit | West Valley Connector (BRT) |

Yet that activity isn't staying local:

- **Few residents work locally.** Only ~15.5% of working residents (12,377 of 79,826) work in Ontario, while
  ~123,552 workers commute in. The figure shows the scale of the mismatch, not its cause: the kinds of jobs
  here, wages, housing costs, where other household members work and commuting preferences can all play a
  part. Scale matters too: a one-point rise in the share needs about 800 more residents working locally (1% of
  79,826).
- **Visitors pass through.** Major destinations are miles apart, and the average stay is ~2.6 days.
- **The business climate has frictions.** In the City's Business Outreach Survey, firms cited
  homelessness (~38%), safety (~36%), rising costs (~31%), and traffic and permitting (~21% each).
- **The City has a direct fiscal stake.** The FY2026–27 General Fund includes ~$216.3M in sales tax and
  ~$18.5M in Transient Occupancy Tax (hotel tax), so more hotel nights and local spending become General
  Fund revenue.

> Figures in this table and list are quoted from the proposal. Where an official source exists, the
> pipeline replaces them (see [Decisions](01_decisions.md#official-sources-first)). Two of them already
> differ from official data and are flagged for correction (see [Conclusions](03_conclusions.md#data-reconciliation)).

## Guiding question

> How can Ontario align its employers, schools, entertainment and logistics assets, and international ties into
> a coordinated strategy that boosts near-term economic activity and connects the next generation to higher-wage
> careers?

## The recommendations being tested

| | Recommendation | Core idea |
|---|---|---|
| **R1** | Ontario Career Pathways & Employer Consortium | A City-convened consortium of employers, colleges, school districts and workforce organizations, built around education → paid internships → local employment → career advancement → resident retention, across five tracks: engineering, logistics, green economy, visitor economy and public services |
| **R2** | A Connected Destination Economy | Joint City–ONT outreach (coordinated event calendars, restaurant and hotel packages, visitor itineraries, a QR-code guide for arriving travelers) plus a two-bus evening transit pilot linking Downtown, Capital City and Toyota Arena, building on the West Valley Connector, to lengthen the average visitor stay |
| **R3** | Clean, Safe & Green Entertainment Districts | A Downtown ambassador pilot managed or sponsored by DOIA with an experienced operator, expanding to Capital City on results; green improvements phased in and linked to Career Pathways |

An earlier draft also included international and employer attraction (R4). It isn't in the current proposal; the
model keeps it only as a possible later phase.

The financial rule: **incremental Year 1 General Fund exposure stays at or under $500,000.** Partners
(employers, DOIA, property owners, venues, hotels, event organizers, tourism programs) share the costs of
components that benefit them, and each phase expands only when its results justify it.

## What this project solves

The proposal makes a logical case. This repository makes it **testable**:

1. **What happens if nothing changes?** Status-quo forecasts of hotel tax, sales tax and the live/work
   share, with likely ranges (the counterfactual).
2. **What must each recommendation achieve to pay for itself?** Break-even targets built only from official tax
   rates, actual revenue and the proposal's stated costs.
3. **What would it take to estimate a return?** Which inputs are still team estimates, and who holds the data, so
   Phase 1 measures the right things.
