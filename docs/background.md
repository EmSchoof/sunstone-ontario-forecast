## The challenge

The **Sunstone Economic Development Challenge @ USC Price** pairs graduate teams with a real public client.
Our client is the **City of Ontario, California** (Economic Development Agency). The deliverable is a
proposal of ranges, funding sources and explicit assumptions rather than false precision. Under the
challenge template it must state a budget, name who pays, and say how success will be measured.

Our proposal, *Connecting Ontario: Building the Inland Empire's Next Generation Economy*, comes from
team members Danny Valencia, Haley Chavez, Emily Schoof and Nathaniel Gallardo (USC Price MPA/MPP).

## The problem

Ontario does not lack economic activity. It has:

| Asset | Scale (as cited in the proposal) |
|---|---|
| Ontario International Airport (ONT) | 7M+ passengers; ~$4.8B regional output and ~24,300 jobs (2024) |
| Ontario Mills | ~12M visits a year |
| Toyota Arena / Capital City | ~1.2M guests a year |
| Ontario Sports Empire | ~1.2M projected visitors a year (City projection) |
| Logistics / industrial base | One of Southern California's largest; FTZ 50; HUB@ONT tenants |
| Housing growth | Ontario Ranch |
| Transit | West Valley Connector (BRT) |

Yet that activity isn't staying local:

- **Residents and jobs are disconnected.** Only ~15.5% of working residents (12,377 of 79,826) work in
  Ontario, while ~123,552 workers commute in.
- **Visitors pass through.** Major destinations are miles apart, and the average stay is ~2.6 days.
- **The business climate has frictions.** In the City's Business Outreach Survey, firms cited
  homelessness (~38%), safety (~36%), rising costs (~31%), and traffic and permitting (~21% each).
- **The City has a direct fiscal stake.** The FY2026–27 General Fund includes ~$216.3M in sales tax and
  ~$18.5M in Transient Occupancy Tax (hotel tax), so more hotel nights and local spending become General
  Fund revenue.

## Guiding question

> How can Ontario use its existing employers, educational institutions, entertainment assets, transportation
> infrastructure and international connections to increase near-term economic activity while creating
> pathways into higher-wage careers for the next generation?

## The recommendations being tested

| | Recommendation | Core idea |
|---|---|---|
| **R1** | Ontario Career Pathways & Employer Consortium | Education → paid internship/apprenticeship → local job → career → staying in Ontario |
| **R2** | Connected Destination Economy (Stay +1) | Turn existing airport, sports, arena and shopping visits into extra hotel nights and multi-stop trips |
| **R3** | Clean, Safe & Green Districts | A Downtown ambassador pilot plus clean/safe/green upgrades, so the district feels safe and active |
| **R4** | International collaboration (later phases) | Use ONT's Taiwan/Mexico service and FTZ 50 to attract and keep employers and university partners |

The financial rule: **incremental Year 1 General Fund exposure stays at or under $500,000.** Partners
(employers, DOIA, venues, hotels, tourism) pay for components that directly benefit them.

## What this project solves

The proposal makes a logical case. This repository makes it **testable**:

1. **What happens if nothing changes?** Status-quo forecasts of hotel tax, sales tax and the live/work
   share, with likely ranges (the counterfactual).
2. **What does each recommendation add on top?** Short-term (FY27–29) and long-term (FY30–36) effects on
   City revenue, City cost, residents working locally and new residents, built on the City's existing investments.
3. **What must be true for it to work?** Which unknown numbers matter most, so Phase 1 measures the right things.
